Explainability for porosity defect inspection

A classifier says whether a camera image looks defective or OK. Explainability
helps an inspector see which image areas contributed to that decision. In this
project, the model also produces a heatmap: brighter areas carry more evidence
for a defect. The camera tool blurs small parts of the image one at a time and
draws approximate rectangles where this changes the classifier's decision most.

This matters because a high defect score alone does not reveal whether the
model looked at a pore, a scratch, the casting edge, a shadow, or the background.
Reviewing the heatmap can expose mistakes caused by lighting, camera position,
or features that happened to correlate with the training labels. It also gives
an inspector a place to check and helps explain disputed results.

The supplied images are labeled only as defective or OK. Nobody marked the
exact defect pixels or boxes. The heatmap is therefore learned indirectly from
whole-image labels. Heatmaps and rectangles are clues about model evidence, not measured
defect boundaries or proof that a pore exists there. Check important results
against the image and a human inspection. To measure localization accuracy or
train a precise detector, obtain human-reviewed defect boxes or masks.
